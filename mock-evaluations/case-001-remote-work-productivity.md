# Case 001 — Remote Work Productivity

**Status:** Completed practice evaluation  
**Evaluation type:** AI response quality assessment  
**Overall finding:** Needs Major Revision

## Task issue

The response generalized a study-specific finding of approximately **24.7%** into a broader statement that remote workers are generally **25% more productive**.

## My evaluation

| Dimension | Score |
|---|---:|
| Accuracy | 3/5 |
| Completeness | 2/5 |
| Relevance | 3/5 |
| Reasoning | 2/5 |
| Instruction-following | 2/5 |
| Clarity | 4/5 |

**Severity:** Major  
**Verdict:** Needs Major Revision

## Key problems identified

1. **Overgeneralization** — a specific study result was presented as a broad conclusion about remote workers generally.
2. **Evidence was not adequately established** — the response did not provide enough support for the strength of its claim.
3. **Undefined measurement** — "25% more productive" does not explain what productivity measure produced the figure.
4. **Reasoning leap** — the response moved from a specific finding to a general conclusion without adequately explaining evidence limits.
5. **Instruction-following issue** — the broad wording should have been challenged or qualified.
6. **One-sided framing** — uncertainty and study-specific limitations were not sufficiently acknowledged.

## Main lesson

A response can contain a real statistic and still be misleading if the statistic is generalized beyond what the evidence supports.

I learned to inspect the relationship between:

**claim → evidence → reasoning → conclusion**

rather than rewarding an answer simply because it sounds informed.

## Evaluator reflection

This case became an early foundation for my later evidence-verification work. It taught me to distinguish **having a number** from **having enough evidence to justify the claim made from that number**.
