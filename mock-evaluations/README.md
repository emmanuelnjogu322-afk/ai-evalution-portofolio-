## What these exercises are teaching me

The purpose of the mock evaluations is not simply to assign scores. I am practicing how to:

- identify the actual task requirements
- separate factual accuracy from fluency
- detect unsupported or overgeneralized claims
- distinguish evidence from assertion
- assess reasoning quality
- apply a rubric consistently
- classify severity
- justify a judgment with specific evidence
- calibrate confidence
- challenge my own first impression
- recognize when an answer needs revision rather than accepting a polished response

## Evaluation loop

**Read task → identify criteria → inspect response → verify important claims → identify failure → assess severity → justify judgment → assign scores/verdict → reflect on calibration**

## Important portfolio principle

A completed evaluation is evidence of **practice**, not evidence of professional client work.

Where a case record is incomplete, I keep the portfolio honest rather than filling gaps with invented details. Future entries can expand the case when the original evaluation artifact is available.

## Progress note

The sequence of cases has helped me move from simply asking *"Is this answer good?"* toward asking:

> **"What specifically makes this answer reliable or unreliable, and what evidence supports my judgment?"**
