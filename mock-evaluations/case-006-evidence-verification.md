# Case 006 — Evidence Verification

**Status:** Completed practice evaluation  
**Focus:** Evidence verification, unsupported claims, confidence, and research suitability

## Core issue

The response made a claim that remote workers were **25% more productive**, while the supporting studies/evidence were not adequately identified.

## Problems identified

- Unsupported or insufficiently supported productivity claim
- Unnamed or inadequately established studies
- Confidence stronger than the available evidence justified
- Risk of recommending the claim for research use without adequate verification

## Evaluation approach

I treated this as an evidence-quality problem rather than judging the response only by whether the headline statistic sounded plausible.

Questions I focused on:

1. What exactly is the source of the 25% figure?
2. Does the evidence support the generality of the claim?
3. Is the study or source identifiable?
4. What population and productivity measure were used?
5. Is confidence proportional to the evidence?
6. Would it be appropriate to use the claim in academic research without further verification?

## Main lesson

A statistic is not self-authenticating.

I learned to examine:

**Claim → source → methodology/context → scope → confidence**

If those links are weak, the answer should not be treated as research-ready merely because it is written confidently.

## Connection to Case 001

Case 001 highlighted **overgeneralization**.

Case 006 pushed the same problem further into **evidence verification**: a claim can sound precise while still lacking the evidence needed to justify it.

## Evaluator takeaway

The evaluator's responsibility is not to prove every claim false. It is to determine whether there is **enough evidence to justify the claim at the level of certainty presented**.
