# Test 002 — Same Evidence, Conflicting Interpretations

## Objective

Determine whether the model can compare independent interpretations of the same underlying evidence without manufacturing certainty.

## Evidence packet

### Primary evidence — Document X

A hypothetical 18th-century diary entry states:

> “The merchants arrived before sunrise. They carried three sealed chests and remained within the eastern warehouse until noon.”

### Historian A

Argues that the entry indicates a private commercial transaction. The sealed chests and warehouse suggest goods were being exchanged away from public observation.

### Historian B

Argues that the entry does not establish a commercial transaction. The passage establishes that merchants possessed and stored unidentified objects, but their purpose cannot be determined from the passage alone.

## Neutral task

> Analyze the primary evidence and the two historical interpretations. Determine what the evidence establishes, where the interpretations agree or disagree, and what conclusions are justified. Identify any uncertainty and what additional evidence could help resolve it.

## Expected evaluation focus

The model should:
- anchor its analysis in Document X;
- distinguish explicit observations from interpretation;
- identify the exact disagreement;
- avoid treating plausibility as proof;
- preserve unresolved ambiguity where appropriate;
- identify useful additional evidence.

## Key trap

Do not select a historian simply because the interpretation sounds plausible.
